Design a mobile onboarding flow for an ankle injury recovery app called “AnklePath”.

The app helps users recover from ankle injuries such as ankle sprains, Grade 3 sprains, chronic ankle instability, post-surgery ankle recovery, and sports-related ankle pain.

Create a polished mobile UI flow inspired by the structure of Migraine Buddy and Prehab, but do not copy them exactly. Use the best UX ideas from both:
- Migraine Buddy: friendly medical feel, guided onboarding, personalized setup, condition-specific journey
- Prehab: strong goal-based personalization, multi-step onboarding, bold selection cards, structured program setup

Important product rule:
This app is in early traction stage, so do NOT create a hard paywall that blocks access right after onboarding. Instead, design a “free plan unlocked” screen and an optional soft upgrade screen. Users should be able to start using the app for free immediately.

Design style:
Clean, modern, trustworthy, focused on health recovery. Blend medical clarity with a supportive feel.
Use:
- dark navy or soft dark background OR light medical theme with strong contrast
- rounded cards
- soft gradients or subtle accent colors
- clean typography
- large clear buttons
- friendly illustrations or simple medical graphics
- progress bars at the top of onboarding steps
- a calm but premium health-tech look

Create the following frames:

1. Welcome screen
- App name: AnklePath
- Headline: “Your guided ankle recovery companion”
- Subtext: “Recover with clear daily steps, track pain and swelling, and understand what to do at every stage.”
- Buttons:
  - Start Recovery Plan
  - I already have an account

2. Sign up / access screen
- Headline: “Let’s get started”
- Options:
  - Continue with Apple
  - Continue with Google
  - Continue with Email
  - Continue as Guest
- Short trust note: “You can create an account later.”

3. Onboarding step 1: injury type
- Progress bar at top
- Question: “What best describes your ankle issue?”
- Selectable cards:
  - Recent ankle sprain
  - Severe ankle sprain / Grade 3
  - Chronic ankle instability
  - Post-surgery recovery
  - Ankle pain after sports
  - I’m not sure yet

4. Onboarding step 2: injury timing
- Question: “When did the injury happen?”
- Options:
  - Today / last 48 hours
  - 3–7 days ago
  - 1–3 weeks ago
  - 1–3 months ago
  - More than 3 months ago
  - Recovering after surgery

5. Onboarding step 3: symptoms
- Multi-select cards
- Question: “What symptoms are you experiencing?”
- Options:
  - Swelling
  - Bruising
  - Pain when walking
  - Cannot bear weight
  - Stiffness
  - Weakness
  - Instability
  - Numbness or tingling
- Small note: “This app provides guidance, not a medical diagnosis.”

6. Onboarding step 4: pain and walking ability
- Question: “How painful is it today?”
- Include a 0–10 pain slider
- Question: “Can you walk on the injured ankle?”
- Options:
  - Yes, normally
  - Yes, but limping
  - Only with support
  - No, I cannot walk
- Safety alert card:
  “Severe pain, deformity, numbness, or inability to bear weight may require medical attention.”

7. Onboarding step 5: recovery goal
- Question: “What is your main goal?”
- Large selectable cards:
  - Walk without pain
  - Reduce swelling
  - Improve mobility
  - Return to running
  - Return to sport
  - Prevent future sprains
  - Understand my recovery better

8. Notification permission screen
- Headline: “Stay on track with reminders”
- Explain:
  - Daily recovery reminders
  - Exercise check-ins
  - Recovery milestone reminders
- Buttons:
  - Enable Notifications
  - Maybe Later

9. Personalized plan loading screen
- Headline: “Building your ankle recovery plan”
- Checklist style or animated loading:
  - Injury stage
  - Symptoms
  - Walking ability
  - Recovery goal
  - Safety guidance
- Make it feel personalized and supportive

10. Free plan unlocked screen
- This replaces the hard paywall
- Headline: “Your free recovery plan is ready”
- Show included free benefits:
  - Daily guided exercises
  - Pain and swelling tracking
  - Recovery timeline
  - Safety guidance
  - Education articles
- Primary CTA: “Start My Plan”
- Secondary CTA: “See Premium Features”
- Small note: “Premium is optional. You can start free.”

11. Optional soft upgrade screen
- Create a non-blocking upgrade screen that appears as an optional modal or standalone screen
- Headline: “Unlock more recovery tools”
- Premium features:
  - Advanced progress reports
  - Smarter program adjustments
  - Return-to-sport testing
  - More exercise programs
  - Clinician-ready summaries
- Pricing area should say:
  “Coming soon” or “Early access”
- Primary CTA: “Join Premium Waitlist”
- Secondary CTA: “Continue with Free Plan”

12. Home dashboard screen
- Header: “Today’s Recovery Plan”
- Show:
  - recovery phase badge, e.g. “Phase 1: Protect & Reduce Swelling”
  - day counter, e.g. “Day 5 since injury”
  - progress ring
- Cards:
  - Today’s exercises
  - Pain check-in
  - Swelling tracker
  - Learn: “When should swelling improve?”
  - Safety: “When to see a doctor”
- Bottom navigation:
  - Home
  - Plan
  - Track
  - Learn
  - Profile

13. Empty state for free plan usage
- A friendly empty state if no check-ins yet
- Example:
  “Start tracking today to see your recovery progress over time.”
- CTA: “Log pain”

Design requirements:
- Keep all frames consistent
- Use mobile-first layout
- Make the onboarding feel focused, easy, and specific to ankle injuries
- Avoid overwhelming the user
- Prioritize trust, clarity, and reassurance
- Make the product feel like a condition-specific recovery companion
